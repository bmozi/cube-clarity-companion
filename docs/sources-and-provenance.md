# Where do these lessons and algorithms come from?

John Briggs · Updated November 25, 2026

These are author-authorized standalone adaptations of material retained outside the reader editions of **How to Master the Cube**, plus original companion practice guidance. The public source of record is [this repository](https://github.com/bmozi/cube-clarity-companion). The private manuscript and production assets are not distributed.

The project’s sticker geometry, prose, diagram layouts, generated target routes, worked traces and tests are original project work. AI assisted drafting, illustration development and computational checks; John Briggs is responsible for the content. Figure files depict exact model states or explicitly labeled structural explanations. Tests do not establish physical execution comfort or learner comprehension.

## Primary references

- [J Perm’s beginner 2×2 tutorial](https://jperm.net/2x2): an optional comparison method; follow its own complete route rather than mixing fragments.
- [Stefan Pochmann’s blindsolving account](https://www.stefan-pochmann.info/spocc/blindsolving/3x3/): historical buffer-swap context. This project derives its own explicit setup/undo and collateral-exchange tables.
- [WCA Regulations](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/), particularly Article B: official blindfold procedure. Checked November 25, 2026; event procedures and the current Regulations govern an actual competition.
- [MIT’s Mathematics of the Rubik’s Cube](https://web.mit.edu/sp.268/www/rubik.pdf): group structure and legal-state background.
- [Rokicki, Kociemba, Davidson and Dethridge’s twenty-move research site](https://www.cube20.org/): half-turn diameter, linked quarter-turn result and published superflip setup. Replay tests do not prove the diameter theorem or shortestness.
- [Richard Korf, AAAI 1997](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1997/AAAI97-109.pdf): optimal search with IDA* and pattern databases. The small teaching solver does not reproduce Korf’s databases or performance.
- [Herbert Kociemba’s two-phase coordinates](https://kociemba.org/math/twophase.htm) and [cubie-level explanation](https://kociemba.org/math/cubielevel.htm): architecture and conventions. The pseudocode is independently expressed; no production backend is included.
- CubeSkills’ [OLL](https://www.cubeskills.com/uploads/pdf/tutorials/oll-algorithms.pdf) and [PLL](https://www.cubeskills.com/uploads/pdf/tutorials/pll-algorithms.pdf) sheets: conventional labels and selected reference-informed routes. The JSON records retain case-level route-selection information and reference links.
- CubeSkills’ [F2L module](https://www.cubeskills.com/tutorials/f2l) and [lookahead progression](https://www.cubeskills.com/blog/lookahead-progression-framework): optional further learning, separate from this project’s original worked examples and model tests.
